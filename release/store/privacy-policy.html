SENTINEL PRIVACY POLICY
Version 3 — 6 November 2026

Sentinel is an Android companion for a Linux PC you are authorised to access.

No developer collection
Sentinel has no developer-operated backend, app account system, advertising,
analytics, tracking or automatic crash-reporting service. The app does not send
connection details, readings, commands or terminal output to the developer.
It does not sell data or use advertising/device identifiers.

Data used to provide the app
You enter a PC hostname/address, SSH port, login, verified host fingerprint and
optional display name. Saved host/login profiles are encrypted using an Android Keystore AES-GCM key and stored in the app's private storage
on your phone until you replace them, erase local app data or uninstall.
Separate monitoring and optional terminal SSH key pairs are generated in Android
Keystore. Private keys are not exported; public keys identify your authorised
connections and may remain in the PC's authorised-key file after uninstall.

When you connect, your configured PC receives your SSH login, public key,
connection address and monitoring requests or terminal commands. SSH encrypts
the connection and Sentinel checks the pinned server fingerprint. DNS and your
chosen network/VPN provider may handle connection metadata under their own
policies. There is no Sentinel relay or cloud service.

The monitoring helper reads permitted system/process metadata: PC hostname,
boot identity, uptime, local account names/UIDs, process names/IDs/resource use,
OS/service state, mount paths/capacity, network interface names/counters and
available hardware sensor readings. It does not read document contents,
browser tabs, process command lines or process environments. Readings are
used to show the connected PC's state. Permissions on the PC limit visibility.
This app does not access your phone's contacts, location, photos or microphone.

Live monitoring readings and terminal scrollback are held in memory, not saved
as history or uploaded by Sentinel. Previous versions' saved status snapshots
are removed on upgrade. Terminal input is not saved as an app command history.
Leaving the terminal or backgrounding the app disconnects its session. Its
in-memory scrollback can remain until cleared or the app process ends; changing
the saved PC clears it. Your PC may separately retain SSH logs, shell history,
command output or files. Commands you run can transmit data to other services;
those destinations are chosen by you and are outside Sentinel's monitoring.

Permissions and choices
Internet access enables SSH and name resolution. Android 17+ local-network
permission enables connections to your configured PC; it is requested before
connections are enabled. Sentinel does not scan for other devices. Monitoring
runs while its relevant screen is visible, with no continuous background alert
service. Notification, location and broad storage permissions are not requested.

You may copy public keys to the system clipboard or export the Linux helper to
a location/provider you choose using Android's file picker. Those copies are
outside Sentinel's private storage and remain until you remove them. Clipboard
and selected storage providers have their own behaviour/policies. Public keys
are not private-key material, but can link connections using the same key.

Retention and deletion
Use Settings > Erase local app data to disconnect and remove saved settings,
notice acknowledgement and both SSH keys and the connection-encryption key. The app closes and requires pairing
again. Android app backup/device transfer is disabled. Uninstalling also removes
the app's private data. No developer account or server-side deletion request is
needed because Sentinel operates no such account or datastore.
Remove the phone's public-key entries on the PC to revoke access. Erasing this
app does not erase PC logs, shell history, exported files or clipboard copies.

Third-party software and platform services
Sentinel uses Android/Jetpack, Kotlin, JSch and Bouncy Castle libraries for UI,
lifecycle and encrypted SSH. No analytics/advertising SDK is included. Android,
Google Play, your PC and your selected network/storage providers may process
data independently under their own terms; this policy describes Sentinel.

Changes and contact
Material changes to the disclosed app behaviour will update this policy and,
where appropriate, require a renewed notice or Android permission request.
For support and privacy questions, use the developer contact published with
Sentinel's Google Play listing. A public contact and hosted copy of this policy
must be provided by the publisher before this build is submitted to Google Play.

Device verification and power actions
Sentinel requires phone fingerprint or screen-lock verification only to reveal
saved connection settings or terminal details. Opening the app, monitoring and
shutdown do not require phone verification. Shutdown requires the PC sudo
password entered fresh for each request. Android handles
that verification; Sentinel never receives your phone PIN or biometric template.
USE_BIOMETRIC enables the system verification prompt. Screenshots and recent-app
previews of the app are blocked. Host/port/login profiles are encrypted in private
phone storage with an Android Keystore AES-GCM key and remembered separately.

Shutdown uses the existing Terminal SSH key and your PC account's existing sudo
permissions. Each request asks for the PC administrator password in a masked field.
This is the PC password, not your phone PIN. It is held only for that request, sent
only to the fingerprint-verified SSH host when its sudo prompt is received, and
never saved in profiles, command text, logs, history, analytics or a developer
service. Raw authentication output is not displayed. Your PC/PAM may independently
record authentication attempts under its existing logging policy.

The app first verifies PC administrator authentication without a power command.
Only an explicitly confirmed shutdown proceeds to a fixed systemctl command,
respecting blocking inhibitors and existing sudo rights. The separate verification
check cannot request shutdown. Password retries and shutdown retries are not
automatic. All PC users and running work can be affected; a lost reply is reported
as uncertain. No new PC helper, software download, sudoers/polkit rule, SSH key
authorisation, service or network change is made for shutdown. Existing monitoring
still uses its previously documented helper. SSH passwords are not stored.
